Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, "What a Fair Comparison of Earth Observation
Foundation Models Actually Looks Like on One Small, Real, Consequential Flood," for
consideration by the International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction.

The manuscript reports a real-data case study comparing two Earth observation
foundation models (Prithvi-EO-2.0, Clay v1.5) against a from-scratch U-Net on a real
Dhaka-area land-cover task and a real 2026 Bangladesh monsoon flood at Dirai,
Sunamganj -- the same event and region your journal's own published work (the
tri-dimensional GIS-AHP flood risk framework for northeastern Bangladesh) has already
identified as a high-priority disaster-risk zone. Rather than a new benchmark or
model, the paper's contribution is a documented, honestly-reported account of what a
single practitioner actually gets when applying these models at the scale a real
local deployment operates at -- including a single-run foundation-model advantage
that did not survive a seed-variance check, and a measured gap between these models'
own published benchmark numbers and their performance on this one real event. On the
real flood, neither foundation model outperformed a classical Otsu threshold.

I believe this work is a good fit for the journal's scope at the intersection of
geospatial methods and disaster risk reduction, and that its explicit focus on
small-sample, single-event, locally consequential evaluation -- rather than
large-scale benchmark performance -- offers a useful, complementary perspective for
practitioners making real deployment decisions.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All code, notebooks, and real experiment run logs are
publicly available (link in the manuscript's Data and Code Availability section) to
support full reproducibility.

Thank you for your consideration.
