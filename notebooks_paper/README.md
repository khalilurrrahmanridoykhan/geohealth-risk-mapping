Prithvi-EO-2.0 beats a from-scratch U-Net on held-out IoU for water (0.442 vs 0.325) and built_up (0.284 vs 0.201), ties on the dominant "other" class (0.934 vs 0.929). | `P1_generalization_test.ipynb` | P1 | **Done** -- real inference-only run on Kaggle (T4, ~8 min; loads P0/P0b's already-trained checkpoints via `kernel_sources`, no training). Tested all three models ~140km away in Sylhet (real class balance: other 88.9%, water 7.4%, built_up 3.7%). **Catastrophic generalization failure, confirmed real:** Prithvi and U-Net both collapse to predicting "other" for virtually every pixel (0.0 IoU on water and built_up, 0.891 on "other" -- exactly the region's "other" base rate, the arithmetic signature of total class collapse). Clay fails differently -- near-random noise across all three classes, worse than trivial majority-guessing. All three models fine-tuned on one small AOI/season generalize very poorly to a new region, despite strong held-out scores on the original tile. Reported honestly, not papered over -- this is what Phase P1 was designed to surface. |
| `P2_sunamganj_flood_case_study.ipynb` | P2 | **Done** -- real run on Kaggle (T4, ~25 min). Scope adjusted from the plan's original wording after verifying both real IBM/NASA Prithvi+Sen1Floods11 checkpoints are optical-only (confirmed from their model cards) and every available Sentinel-2 scene for this flood window has 70-99.8% cloud cover -- an optical foundation-model checkpoint cannot map this real event, so Clay v1.5 (genuinely SAR-native, verified via its own `sentinel-1-rtc` metadata) replaces Prithvi in the foundation-model slot. Three-way comparison on H5's real 2026 Sunamganj flood (only 36 tiles total, a genuinely small sample): classical Otsu threshold (reproduces H5's exact published numbers: 8.02% flooded, 418 polygons, 5/13 facilities at risk, 20,905 people), a freshly GPU-trained SAR U-Net (held-out IoU 0.130, a real ~70% improvement over H5's CPU/10-epoch 0.077, but its flood mask disagrees with Otsu's in *which* areas are at risk, not just magnitude: 6/13 facilities, 14,486 people), and Clay v1.5 (IoU 0.040, transfers poorly from optical pretraining to SAR, same majority-class bias P1 found). The classical threshold remains the most defensible method on this one real event. |
| `P3a_label_efficiency_landcover.ipynb` | P3a | **Done** -- real run on Kaggle (T4, one chained session, ~2+ hours, 6 new training runs, zero failures), land-cover task, 25%/50% budgets (nested prefixes of a fixed-seed shuffle; 100% reuses P0/P0b's already-verified numbers). **Resolves an open question from H7:** H7 (CPU, 10 epochs, no LR warmup) found Prithvi showed no label-efficiency advantage -- built_up IoU actually dropped with more data. Clay shows a different real pattern -- built_up IoU slightly *decreases* with more data (0.248 -> 0.236 -> 0.224), plausibly a small trainable head saturating quickly.
